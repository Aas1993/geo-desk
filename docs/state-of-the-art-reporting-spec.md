## C. The standard every product meets

1. **As-of discipline.** Every product states its as-of date, the age of each source and a freshness mark; news items are dated and sourced; a number carries its year.
2. **Bottom line first**, then the "so what" for the UAE's interests in one paragraph, then the decision requested.
3. **Angle coverage matrix** (B): covered, thin, not found, per angle.
4. **Options with trade-offs and a red team**: at least three courses, the strongest case against the recommended one, assumptions and what would break each.
5. **Scenarios and tripwires**: base, upside and downside, each with a likelihood band and the indicators and thresholds that move the plan.
6. **An implementable plan**, not a list of ideas: objective, initiative, owner (a named role), start and finish dates, measure and target, resources ("not estimated" when unknown), dependencies, risks, the decision gate that releases the next step; horizons of 30 days, 100 days, one year and three years; a sequencing view.
7. **The UAE statecraft playbook for the country** (D), consistent with the plan.
8. **Coalition and friends plan**: who to bring in (countries, blocs, institutions, companies), what each is offered and asked, the votes and the seats that matter.
9. **Influence plan**: soft-power and hard-power levers (policy level), the investment and trade levers, the logistics and corridor play, the technology, space and energy play, and the narrative.
10. **Monitoring**: indicators with thresholds, review dates, owners; "what changed since the last report" (a diff).
11. **Sources, confidence and the boundary**: sources named and graded; likelihood and confidence separated; every move a proposal for a human decision; open, lawful, consent-based engagement only; no covert, coercive or targeting content.
12. **Independent audit** before publishing (the reviewer pair), cost ceiling US$1.00 per run, click-started.
13. **Presentation**: a bottom-line header with quality and auditor chips, structured data drawn as tiles, bars, timelines, matrices and maps, interactive filters, collapsible sections, Arabic and right-to-left, and a PDF with the same visuals.

## D. The UAE statecraft playbook for a country

A per-country section titled "The UAE geopolitical statecraft playbook for <country>", rule-built for every country from the Case File (no model cost) and enriched by the agents where a product runs. Sections:

1. **Course and posture** (deepen, stage, hedge, hold) with the rule and the value, ability and risk behind it.
2. **Interests, ranked**: what the UAE wants (political, security, economic, standing), marked stated or inferred; and what the other side wants.
3. **Instruments in order**: footprint and headroom per instrument; which to use first and why.
4. **Influence ledger**: standing against each rival and the gap to close.
5. **Friends and coalition plan**: partners to align, the blocs and votes, mediation roles.
6. **Economic, trade and investment play**: CEPA use, funds, ports, sectors, the announced-against-delivered tracker.
7. **Corridor and logistics play**: the silk road legs, assets held, gaps, first moves.
8. **Energy, technology and space play** where the country offers or needs it.
9. **Soft-power and narrative play**: education, culture, aid, media, tourism, diaspora.
10. **Security cooperation (policy level) and consular posture.**
11. **Diplomatic calendar**: visits, summits, elections, treaty dates, deadlines.
12. **Red lines and the boundary**; legal and sanctions screen.
13. **Triggers and tripwires**: what upgrades or downgrades the posture; the indicators.
14. **90-day, one-year and three-year implementation plan** (C6) with owners.

It appears in: the ambassador's reports (intelligence and mission strategy), the Ministry's council, the desk officer's brief, the Bird's eye report and the PDFs of all of them, the Country Brief and the President's brief.

## E. The comparison page in detail

**Purpose.** Put two to four countries side by side, see where each stands on the fourteen angles and on the UAE's relationship, rank them for a purpose, and ask agents for a specific comparative analysis that ends in a report.

**Select.** A multi-picker (2 to 4) with presets: a country's neighbours, its rivals' favourites, the corridor chain, a region, "the countries I have briefed".

**Free, rule-built comparison (no model).**
- *Scorecard*: a radar or grouped bars over the fourteen angles (where data exists) and the six lenses of the Case File; the leader per metric highlighted; angles with no data shown as gaps.
- *Metric table*: macro, trade with the UAE, investment, partnership index, UN voting, agreements, CEPA, visits in 12 months, flights, instruments footprint and headroom, governance, rivals' shares; sortable, toggles per group, small multiples for time series (trade, growth), units normalised.
- *Relationship view*: the UAE's position in each (embassy, committee, agreements, last visits), side by side.
- *Ranking by purpose*: adjustable weights (sliders) for a purpose (corridor partner, investment destination, security partner, diplomatic bloc-building, soft-power return); a ranked list that updates live, with the contribution of each metric shown, and what is not held.

**Agents for specific analysis.** A question box with ready questions: which is the better partner for X; where should the UAE put capital first and why; the risk-adjusted ranking; the order to engage; what the shared rivals and the shared neighbours mean; trade-offs and what each country wants from the UAE; a corridor between them. The Desk agent router plans the work: per country, a country brief (free if on file) or a desk directive, then one comparative writer; capped at US$1.00 per run, at most four runs, the total shown, started only by an in-page confirmation.

**Report.** A comparative report in the format asked (decision paper, matrix, memo, table), from the statecraft and geopolitical angle, with the playbook line for each country, the ranking, the trade-offs, the recommended sequence and the implementation plan; PDF with the same visuals; saved in the Archive.

**Checks.** No invented figure; gaps shown; every claim traceable to the table or a department text; labelled proposals.

## G. Data and news

- Country news is tagged by angle (political, security and military, economy and trade, energy, technology and space, logistics and corridors, society and soft power) so each product reads current developments for every angle and the page can filter by them.
- Official sources first (WAM, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the country's own ministries and media), then wires and regional outlets; every item dated and attributed.
- Datasets the platform holds are refreshed on their own schedule; each product states their vintage.

## H. How it is checked

- The quality gate (`src/lib/quality/gate.ts`) checks angle coverage, the plan's fields (owner, date, measure) and the playbook sections; the independent reviewers test them.
- Tests assert the playbook exists for every country in the Case File, the coverage matrix is present in each product, and the plans have owners and dates.
- Paid runs stay under US$1.00; skipped chiefs or auditors are failures; truncated writers are failures.
